## Tools

- [**Claude Duet**](#aff-claude-duet): Anthropic's new app where Claude works on its own computer in the cloud, messages you first, and asks before it acts
- [**Zernio**](#aff-zernio): a scheduling service that posts to TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and YouTube for you, and shows the stats
- [**Notion**](#aff-notion): a shared to-do board where every video sits in a column like "needs recording" or "approved"
- [**Windsor.ai**](#aff-windsor): a service that collects your social stats in one place
- [**VidIQ**](#aff-vidiq): another service that collects your stats, built around YouTube
- [**Google Docs**](#aff-google-docs): one document that says who you are and what you want
- [**GitHub**](#aff-github): where he stores his writing rules

## What you'll build

A social media manager that lives in your phone.

It tells you how last week went on every platform.
It tells you what to make next.
It drafts your LinkedIn posts in your voice.
It makes your Instagram reel covers.
It moves videos along your content board, which tells your editors what to work on.

You still approve everything.

## The story

Shaz Mathew runs an agency that does personal branding and social media for clients.

> We have generated more than seven figures for our clients through personal branding and social media.

Claude Duet is Anthropic's new app. Claude works on its own computer in the cloud, messages you first, and asks before it acts. It's a research preview for US Pro and Max plans.

He turned it into the social media manager he says he'd otherwise pay "thousands of dollars a month" for.

And he runs it from his phone.

## One chat per platform

Duet lets you open separate chats, called channels.

> Think of channels as specific chats to achieve a single goal.

> The mistake many people make is doing everything in one long chat.

His setup:

- one for YouTube
- one for short videos (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook)
- one for LinkedIn
- one for brand deals and partnerships
- one general personal-branding chat that plans across all of them

The general chat is the boss of the others.
That's where he asks things like how to plan a new newsletter, or whether to try Reddit.

## First, teach it who you are

Before anything else, ask Duet for a three-sentence summary of your brand and your goals.

If it can't answer well, it doesn't know enough yet. Every script, title and post will be generic.

His fix is connecting Claude to his files.
He's a heavy Claude user and keeps everything in GitHub, which Claude can read. That's also where his writing rules live.

The simpler version he recommends: one Google Doc with who you are, what your company does, how long you've been at it, your goals and your background. Connect Google Docs to Claude and it can read it.

Add examples of your voice too. Anything you've written or recorded.

## The weekly stats report

Duet can't log into your social accounts.

> Claude cannot connect directly to any of your social networks.

So it needs a go-between: a service that is connected to your accounts and collects the stats in one place. He names Windsor AI and VidIQ as options. His scheduler, Zernio, also shows stats.

Then he asks for the last seven days of short videos across TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, compared with the week before.

It comes back with numbers per platform, where the views came from, and what to make next. In his case: make more videos like the ones that worked.

> A good assistant tells you what to do next

## The content board

His short videos live on a shared board in Notion.
Each video sits in a column: needs a script, needs recording, needs editing, ready for review, approved.

A separate automation connects that board to Zernio.
Anything moved to "approved" gets scheduled and posted.

Duet can move the cards.
So from his phone, on a walk, he says "move the first two videos that need a script to needs recording," and they move.

Moving a card is also how his team knows what to edit.

He hasn't posted a video himself in a long time:

> Claude does it for me

His claim covers the whole short-video setup, with Duet as one piece of it:

> It probably takes me 30 minutes a week to do all my short-form content, which equates to about 90 posts a month

## LinkedIn posts and reel covers

In the LinkedIn chat, he asked for a post about his latest YouTube video.

Duet pulled the video's transcript and ran it through his LinkedIn writing rules.
Those rules say every post gets a keyword, a P.S. at the end, and a second hook to choose from. The draft followed all three.

In the short-video chat, he asked for a cover for his latest Instagram reel.

Duet read what the reel was about and built a cover from his thumbnail kit: his face, his company name, on brand.

> I wouldn't be ashamed to use it.

He said he'd still clean up a few things.

## Never let it run blind

His last step, and the one he says matters most.

> Never let this work blindly, especially for your personal brand.

Someone has to check what gets posted, whether that's you or a team member.

> This is intended to increase the speed and efficiency with which we work, not so much to replace absolutely everything.

He has seen what happens when something goes out that doesn't sound like you. It does more harm than good.

## How it works

1. Open one Duet chat per platform, plus one general chat to plan from.
2. Write one Google Doc about your business, goals and voice, and connect it to Claude.
3. Connect a service that collects your social stats in one place.
4. Ask for last week's numbers per platform, compared with the week before, plus what to make next.
5. Connect a scheduling service so Duet can post for you.
6. Put your videos on a shared to-do board, and have anything marked "approved" go to the scheduler.
7. Write down your rules for each platform's posts, and have Duet follow them.
8. Read everything before it goes out.

## The business angle

Most owners who pay a social media manager on a monthly retainer are paying for four things.
Reports. Ideas. Drafts. Posting.

This setup covers a lot of all four.

The plain truth:

His editors still edit.
He still approves.
A person still checks every post.

What changes is the coordination.
No status meetings, no "what should I post this week" emails, no logging into four apps to see how last week went.

## Who should steal this idea

Owners whose face is the brand.

Coaches, consultants, agency owners, real estate agents, local experts who post on more than one platform.

Especially if you already have an editor or a VA and you're the bottleneck telling them what's next.

## How hard is it

Intermediate.

The chats and the context doc are easy.
Wiring the scheduler and the board together is the hard part. He calls it "a somewhat complex setup." Connecting Zernio means making a key in its settings and handing it to Claude.

Cost: a Claude Pro or Max plan for Duet.
The scheduler, the board and the stats service are separate bills. He didn't give prices, but says he picked Zernio partly for its price, and that he's not sponsored.

## Gotchas and tips

### It sometimes gets stuck

> I just realized that it sometimes gets stuck.

His reel cover stalled until he nudged it.
Check your Duet task list if something is taking too long.

### Folders are web only

On the web, his channels sit neatly inside a personal-branding folder.
The phone app has no folders yet, so it's a flat list.

### Context and data are most of the work

He says giving Claude context and access to your stats is almost 80% of the work.
If Duet can't describe your brand back to you in three sentences, fix that before anything else.

### Check what "approved" does

If moving a card to "approved" posts it, then a voice command can post it.
Know which columns publish before you start talking to your board.

### Keep a human on review

Everything that goes out still represents you.
