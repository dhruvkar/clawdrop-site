## Tools

- [**Claude Duet**](#aff-claude-duet): Anthropic's new app where Claude works on its own computer in the cloud, messages you first, and asks before it acts. A research preview for US Pro and Max plans
- [**Claude in Chrome**](#aff-claude-chrome): an add-on for the Chrome browser that lets Claude click around websites for you, here so it can draft and post on LinkedIn
- [**Gmail**](#aff-gmail): his email, connected so Duet can flag messages that need an answer
- [**LinkedIn**](#aff-linkedin): where the posts go
- [**Audible**](#aff-audible): Amazon's audiobook store, where the book is sold

## What you'll build

A marketing teammate that lives in a phone app.

You give it a task in plain English.
It drafts LinkedIn posts from your latest podcast episode.
It watches the web for mentions of your product, every day.
It researches your product page and tells you how to sell more.
It makes the pictures and short videos for the posts.

You answer it like a text message. It keeps working while you do something else.

## The story

The video is from Marketing Against the Grain, HubSpot's marketing podcast.

The co-host has early access to Claude Duet.
He also co-wrote a book called *Loop* with his co-host Kieran, and they just released the audiobook.

So he uses his own podcast and his own book as the demo.
Everything happens live, in about 20 minutes.

## Five posts, and a catch

His first task:

> turn my most recent podcast episode into uh series of LinkedIn posts. This is the classic content repurposing.

Duet came back with five LinkedIn drafts from episode 466.
Then it asked a question:

> That's Kieran's episode with Saj. Should I keep these or redo them from episode 465?

He wasn't on the most recent episode. He just forgot.

> So I said my most recent podcast, but I wasn't actually in the most recent episode.

He told it to redo them from 465. That one was about the marketing books that shaped the hosts.

A VA would have written five posts about the wrong episode.

## A daily watch for the book

While he was adding the first task, Duet suggested a second one.

> set up a daily watch for mentions of the Loop book online.

It turned that into a task that runs every day.

> each new mention that comes out comes with a link and an idea of how to engage.

> You could do the same thing for your product, right?

## Three ways to sell the audiobook

While the posts were being redone, he asked for a plan:

> Can you build me a plan of three potential ways to get more buyers to the audio version of the Loop book on Amazon and Audible?

Duet went and browsed the audiobook on Amazon: the price, the listing, everything on the page.

It came back with three ideas:

1. Play a 10 to 15 minute chapter as a bonus podcast episode that ends with "hear the rest on Audible."
2. Get the first 50 ratings by asking the publisher for Audible promo codes for their ambassador group and superfans.
3. Add a "listen on Audible" button to HubSpot's Loop page.

His reaction:

> These are not hard things, but they're things that I just wasn't thinking of.

> I didn't even know you could get audible program promo codes.

He told it to draft the bonus episode. It was still drafting when the video ended.

## The visuals

He picked post two, about a classic copywriting book and the idea of "verisimilitude": the appearance of truth.

Then he asked for two things:

1. A short animated video explaining the idea.
2. The frame from the episode where they show the book's text on screen.

Duet downloaded the episode and found the frame itself.

> I'm traveling, so I don't have the book with me. I would have had to wait. Then I would have had to dig out the book from my library. I would have had to find that page on verisimilitude, pick the picture, upload to LinkedIn.

He played the video on camera.

> This is a great video. I'm going to put it in a LinkedIn post.

## Then it needed the browser

To post on LinkedIn, Duet had him install the Claude add-on for Chrome.

> So one of the things you'll also find with these proactive agents is that there's real setup work. Like you need the connectors with your Gmail, with your other tools. You need to connect it with your browser and and everything else.

You never see a post go live in the video.
And there are no audiobook sales numbers yet. The plan is only a plan so far.

## How it works

1. Open Duet and make a thread for your marketing. He calls his threads "agents with tasks."
2. Add a task in plain English: "turn my most recent episode into LinkedIn posts."
3. Answer its questions. It will check things with you before it goes ahead.
4. Ask for a schedule on anything you want repeated, like a daily watch for your brand or product.
5. While one task runs, start another. It works on several at once.
6. Pick the draft you like and ask for the visuals to go with it.
7. Connect Chrome so it can work on LinkedIn for you.

## The business angle

Two bills here.

The first is the content VA or agency you pay to turn each episode into social posts. Duet drafted five, caught the wrong episode, and made the visuals while he talked.

The second is the marketing consultant you'd hire to answer "how do I sell more of this?" Duet read the product page and came back with three cheap, specific ideas. He liked all three.

The brand-monitoring tool is a third, smaller one.

You still pick the post, check the facts, and decide what goes out. That part doesn't get replaced.

## Who should steal this idea

Owners who make content and don't have time to cut it up.

Podcasters, authors, coaches, course creators, consultants with a YouTube channel.

Anyone with one product they wish more people bought.

## How hard is it

Set it up in an afternoon.

The tasks are plain English. The setup is connecting your email, your browser and your other tools.

Cost: he's on Claude's top-tier plan.

> I don't think Duet would be that valuable if you were on the basic version of Claude cuz I think you'd run through your allowance of usage super super quick.

There's no separate Duet bill. It runs on your normal Claude account and its usage.

## Gotchas and tips

### It burns through usage

> They use a lot of tokens.

His whole 20-minute demo ran on the top plan. On a cheaper plan, expect to hit your limit fast.

### Say which episode

"Most recent" was the wrong episode. Duet caught it. Don't count on that every time. Name the episode.

### The setup is real

Email, browser and other tools each need connecting. Budget for it before the first useful week.

### It isn't his first pick

He has access to every app in this category. Duet is his second choice.

> the one I'm using the most is Grok Bot.

He says Grok Bot lets him securely store API keys (the logins for other software) and credit card details, and he likes its separate bots for separate jobs. He calls part of it "personal preference."

### Waitlist only

Duet is a research preview. You may have to join the waitlist.
